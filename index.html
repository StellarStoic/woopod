Library & subscriptions6 capabilities
Available

Direct RSS & Atom subscriptions

Paste a secure feed URL or supported Apple Podcasts link, read the full publisher show description and preview playable episodes with artwork and publication dates, then subscribe beside the show image. A subscribed show offers an unsubscribe confirmation. Search within the preview to filter that show’s episodes. Discover matches appear first without repeating in the rest of the list. The publisher feed remains the source of truth.

Available

Complete, refreshable episode lists

Pull down from the top of Library to check subscriptions with live progress. New entries merge without discarding older episodes omitted by shortened feeds. Opening a podcast starts at its header; Search this podcast filters its episodes, and episode cards show their length when available. The sort icon beside Search this podcast switches between newest and oldest first; the choice is backed up. Android Back or a tap on the selected Library button returns to the Library or Archived list.

Available

Archive without unsubscribing

Move a quiet subscription out of the everyday Library while preserving its catalog, progress, artwork, offline files, queue entries and OPML export. Restore it at any time.

Available

Artwork that arrives quickly

List thumbnails, preview art and Player art use separate sizes and app-private caching. Import a local image to override a show’s cover; restore the publisher image later. In Appearance, turn on Player artwork glow for a soft, fading reflection of the current episode or chapter cover. Its shape changes slowly while playing and holds still when paused. The setting is backed up.

Available

Per-podcast settings

Hold a Library podcast for Info, Pin to top, Priority, Keep latest episodes or Unsubscribe. For offline retention, use the 0–9 slider or enter a value up to 100 and press Done. Library and Archived cards show priority numbers; use Show priorities in the Library menu to hide or show them. Podcast settings include artwork, speed, a skip-silence switch and independent intro and ending skip times. The skip settings apply when an episode starts, require a known duration and are included in backups; resuming keeps your position. Library’s three-dot menu offers Bulk select for applying priority, silence, volume, speed, offline retention and archive changes to selected shows. Settings and menu order are backed up.

Available

Intentional deletion

The red Danger zone asks twice, offers a full backup and clearly separates subscription deletion from queue, progress, appearance and identity.

Listening & playback11 capabilities
Available

Background playback & resume

Media3 keeps audio playing outside the app. Playback pauses when media volume reaches zero. Position checkpoints restore listening after restart, and the mini-player returns you to the full Player and shows the remaining listening time at the current speed. Its long episode title and the podcast name in Player share Playlist’s title scroll speed setting; 0 stops movement on all three.

Available

Precise controls

The central play/pause button slowly changes between soft, randomized blob shapes. Hold it for two seconds to turn it red and reveal Gentleface’s original trash-drop animation; tap it to move the current episode to the Bin, or tap elsewhere to cancel. If playback was active and Autoplay is on, the first unplayed episode continues. Hold rewind or forward for two seconds to move to the previous or next chapter. Tap either timer to cycle through played or remaining time, percentages, minutes and seconds. Time values use the current playback speed, and both choices are backed up. Drag the position dot immediately or tap a point to seek. A swipe starting elsewhere on the progress bar changes pages; cancelled gestures do not change playback. During buffering, the timeline stays visible and “Buffering…” temporarily replaces the left time display. Retry and playback errors stay visible.

Available

Speed, globally or per show

Adjust from 0.5× to 2.5× in Player’s top-right menu. A podcast can retain its own default and apply it as episodes change.

Available

Standalone Playlist

The episode count includes a tappable remaining-time total in hours, days or minutes; unknown durations are noted. Cards show listening progress as a translucent theme fill. Played episodes retain a subtle full-card highlight while a separate fill shows their current replay progress. Choose an animated playing edge in Appearance. Hold an episode handle for 1.5 seconds to reorder, or use Bulk select to mark, favourite or remove episodes and change their podcasts' priority, silence, boost or offline retention.

Available

Played history that stays useful

Mark episodes manually or let completion do it. In a podcast’s settings, mark all saved episodes as played after confirming. Played episodes in Library and Archived show a theme-tinted background and their played icon; Discover can identify previously heard shows. Durable local history survives unsubscribe and can be reversed without changing progress.

Available

A Player that fits

The poster adapts to available height so the episode title, timeline, transport buttons and action rail remain together on one non-scrolling screen. Tap a bookmark marker on the timeline to edit a Markdown note. Optional shake-to-bookmark saves a chosen number of seconds before the gesture, and its settings and notes are included in full backups. Tap the podcast name to open its Library page.

Available

Sort your playlist

Sort by publication date, podcast priority, duration, download date, podcast name, publisher network or listening progress. Progress is the percentage of the current replay listened; episodes with unknown duration stay last. Tap the same sort again to reverse it; hold and drag the sort choices to reorder the menu. Set podcast priority from 0 to 9 in podcast settings, the Library podcast long-press menu, or an episode’s Playlist long-press menu. Zero means no priority; 1 is highest, 9 lowest, and unprioritized shows sort last. See how many subscriptions use each priority as you choose it. Priorities and menu order are included in full backups. Network grouping requires a publisher identifier in the podcast feed; unavailable values stay at the end.

Available

Timestamped bookmark notes

Save a private thought at the current playback time, using the five-second rewind and play/pause controls to find the right moment. Browse All bookmarks from Library’s menu, view a single podcast’s bookmarks on its Library page, or search them locally in Discover. The editor offers Save, Clip and Delete; a clip starts at the bookmark time with its title as the initial public note. Bookmark notes and episode metadata are included in full backups.

Partial

Create a clip link

In Player, tap Scissors. Drag the timeline’s start/end handles, select Start or End, then slide the ruler for fine adjustment. Enter exact times reveals optional seconds or mm:ss input with millisecond precision; Use current position applies to the selected endpoint. Choose any valid portion of the episode—there is no clip-length cap. Woopod reads the actual episode length first; the timeline, fine ruler and typed times stop at that end. Exact times display as mm:ss or hh:mm:ss, and length reads in hours/minutes/seconds. Clip boundaries use the original audio timestamps; when the current episode uses another playback speed, equivalent Player timer positions are shown. The clip preview plays at normal speed. Listen with restart/play-pause or check the final five seconds. A thin green playhead follows the audio on the slider in both the editor and preview, remaining at its position when paused. Create clip opens an editable local preview; Share and Copy share link are available there once sharing details are ready. Comments allow up to 280 Unicode characters and 1,024 UTF-8 bytes. The link includes the feed address and public comment, so use public podcasts only. Older episodes automatically recover their feed from saved details or podcast directories and cache it for next time. If recovery fails, the visible header offers feed entry while local preview still works. Browser playback depends on the companion deployment; creating a link does not post a message or upload audio.

Partial

Player action rail

V4V opens the episode’s payment-service links, other support links and crypto destinations in a large window with visual separation and no group headings. After a successful feed check finds no value address, you can open an editable email draft asking the podcaster about support; it includes Lightning, funding-link and 60/40 guest split examples. Woopod does not send the email itself. In You → Appearance, choose V4V, Bitcoin, a currency symbol, a piggy bank or a coin for this button; your selection is included in backups. Hold any Player action icon for 1.5 seconds and drag left or right to reorder bookmarks, share, support, clips and comments; the order is backed up. The fixed Boost action prefers a connected NWC wallet. External-wallet alternatives for Lightning addresses remain in the top-right recipient overflow menu. A small info icon beside it explains the route and shows selectable XML reconstructed from saved public feed fields, with any alternative address shown separately; this is not the complete original XML and opening it makes no payment request. Direct node payments require a compatible connected wallet. When a node recipient’s published name contains one Lightning address or LNURL-pay code, Woopod can validate its payment service and use an invoice for that same share. The confirmation shows the alternative, service domain and description; the original split weights are preserved. A valid payment service does not prove ownership of the original node. The payment review allocates the publisher portion among routes supported by the connected wallet, preserving relative published split weights and showing excluded recipients before confirmation. Signed boost messages additionally require a compatible recipient and discussion; ordinary split payments do not attach a signed message. Direct node payments without custom routing records are available when the connected wallet advertises keysend; signed node boost messages remain unsupported, and nodes requiring routing records need a validated invoice alternative or are excluded with an explanation. Real-wallet compatibility is still being tested. Ordinary invoices can use a signed BOLT-11 description or a matching service metadata hash; exact amount, signature, network and expiry are checked. Signed boost invoices always require the signed-request hash. Valid LNURLs in an address field are recognized even when a feed labels them as nodes or Lightning addresses. Add a payment note sends a LUD-12 message to supported podcast payment services; it is shown before confirmation and is distinct from a signed public comment. Each included podcast recipient must accept the note, and the Woopod share receives no note. Preparation errors identify the failing recipient and service, amount, note or invoice step. A valid Lightning address alone does not identify a compatible public episode discussion or signed recipient profile; signed public boost messages require that extra information. Public episode comments can still be posted separately. The comments screen offers a support shortcut even when no comments exist. The comment icon opens public episode conversations using Fountain-compatible signed notes and standard podcast identity tags. Read and reply with your connected account; relay confirmations are shown individually. Podcast discussions are available from the podcast page and podcast settings. Episode details has Podcast Info and Episode Comments icons beneath the publication date. Podcast Info has a copy icon beside its RSS feed address. Pull down the episode list to refresh its feed. Publisher discussions remain separately accessible. Share opens Android’s share sheet with a full-episode Woopod player link. The podcast page also has a Share icon for its Woopod podcast page, with expandable episode details and links to the full player. Woopod can also open shared podcast, episode and clip links when Android routes them to the app. Clips play within their selected times; automatic routing requires the release domain verification setup. Shared podcast, episode and clip links open on woopod.com with social preview cards where supported. Scissors creates unlimited-length timed clips with native listening and an editable preview before sharing. The browser player plays within the selected clip boundaries; visibility in other podcast apps can vary.

Available

Descriptions & chapters

Player follows the current chapter title and artwork. In Appearance, choose a straight, zigzag or wave progress line; the shaped styles follow chapter lengths and the choice is backed up. A link icon on the artwork means the current chapter has a publisher link; tap the artwork to open it. Open Chapters for a nearly full-screen list with artwork, starting times and play controls. Its scissors button opens the clip maker with the chapter title and an editable range ending one second before the next chapter, or at the episode end for the final chapter. The Player menu can be reordered by holding its handles; its order is backed up. Inline chapters, timestamps and Podcasting 2.0 JSON chapter files are supported.

Transcripts & listening context

Open publisher transcripts in Player while keeping playback controls visible. Timed WebVTT, SRT and JSON follow playback; HTML and plain text are readable without timing. Scroll manually to pause following. Transcripts load on demand; embedded media chapters are still planned.

Audio tuning7 capabilities
Available

Skip silence

You → Audio sets the default. Player's switch and individual or bulk podcast settings can override it per show; choose App default to remove an override. Changes apply to streamed and downloaded episodes and are included in backups.

Available

Rewind after interruption

Choose Off or rewind 0.5–3 seconds in half-second steps when playback resumes after a temporary audio-focus interruption. Notifications that play without interrupting audio do not trigger it. The setting is included in backups.

Available

Sound between episodes

Choose the built-in chime or an audio file in You → Audio. The sound plays when playback automatically advances, before the next episode starts; manual selection and replay do not trigger it. Backups remember the choice but do not include audio files, so choose a custom file again on a new device.

Available

Volume boost

Raise a podcast's quiet recordings by 0–12 dB from Player's menu or Audio settings. Its saved level follows every episode of that show, including background playback, and is included in backups. Peak limiting protects against digital clipping; strong boost can reduce dynamics. Automatic loudness leveling remains future work.

Available

Mono audio

Combine all channels so either ear receives the full conversation. Mono takes precedence over surround and disables its slider while enabled.

Available

Five-band equalizer & presets

Choose from 20 saved curves: Flat, Speech, Warm Voice, Vocal Clarity, Rock, Pop, Jazz, Classical, Acoustic, Electronic, Dance, Hip-Hop, R&B, Metal, Bass Boost, Treble Boost, Bass Cut, Treble Cut, Loudness and Late Night. Adjusting a band marks the curve Custom. The five sliders span −12 to +12 dB; presets keep your other audio controls unchanged.

Available

Bass

A separate 0–12 dB bass control works with the equalizer on or off. Headroom compensation keeps tonal changes from blindly increasing already loud peaks.

Available

Surround / stereo width

Widen stereo sound from 0–100%, best heard through headphones. Centred voices stay centred; mono recordings cannot gain missing stereo detail. Reset audio returns all these controls to their original defaults.

Offline & cleanup5 capabilities
Available

Download any episode

Save audio for local playback. Download from Library, episode details, Discover or a show preview to add the episode to Playlist. A compact status strip opens transfer details, including speed and estimated time when available. While downloading, episode cards show a subtle glowing border that follows progress; an outline indicates activity if the file size is unknown. If Android rejects a long redirect chain, Woopod can resolve up to 20 secure redirects and retry on a permitted connection. Ready files replace the network source; partial, failed and binned files are not played as downloads.

Available

Keep the latest N

Set 1–100 newest episodes per podcast, or zero to disable. Replacements must finish before older files move to the Bin, and the current Player item is protected.

Available

Network rules

Wi-Fi-only is the default. Cellular can be allowed deliberately; roaming downloads remain disabled.

Available

Flexible checks

Choose manual, 1/3/6/12/24-hour intervals, or a daily local time. Android may delay background work for battery or network conditions.

Available

A recoverable Playlist Bin

Playlist’s three-dot menu offers Update and an Autoplay switch, also available in Settings. After an episode finishes, Autoplay chooses the first unplayed episode in the current Playlist order, including after reordering, and stops when none remain. Played episodes can still be selected manually. Completed automatic downloads are appended without interrupting playback. Its long-press action “Remove and delete” moves an episode to the Bin. In Settings, choose whether fully listened episodes move there automatically. Downloaded audio and streaming episodes can be restored to Playlist. Restoring an episode resets playback to the beginning while preserving its played history. A shared slider in Settings and Bin keeps files for 15 min, 30 min, 1 h, 6 h, 12 h, 1 d, 3 d or 7 d.

User & identity7 capabilities
Available

Create or import an identity

Create a local account with a display name and random DiceBear Stack avatar, or sign in with an existing nsec or hexadecimal private key. Local keys are encrypted on the device and kept when you sign out; saved accounts are identified on the sign-in screen. Woopod backups exclude private keys, so keep a separate copy. Importing a key does not publish a profile.

Available

Explicit secret backup

The nsec stays hidden and red until requested. A secure dialog explains that there is no password reset, supports user-triggered copying and recommends a password manager.

Available

Connect an Android signer

Amber/NIP-55 connection keeps the signing key in the signer app. Woopod can display the public identity without silently requesting event-signing permission.

Available

Portable public profile and relays

Verified profiles are read from Woopod, purplepag.es and any additional relays you add under You → Nostr. Locally created accounts can publish to them with per-relay acknowledgements; public comments also use your added relays. The section explains what is public, what remains local and how to control your data. Additional relay settings are backed up; encrypted relay backups remain on Woopod.

Partial

Connect an NWC wallet

In You → Connect NWC wallet, paste a connection string or use the QR icon to scan one from an external wallet that supports Nostr Wallet Connect. Scanning runs on your device. The connection is encrypted locally and excluded from backups; you can run a read-only connection check. You can set a Woopod support share for future boosts, with a 1% minimum; this preference is included in backups. The share is included in the boost total and rounded up to a whole sat. A Boost button in your selected theme color stays fixed and visible while you scroll the episode’s recipient cards. It opens a compact amount-and-recipient review: theme color means the connected wallet supports the route, red means it does not, and neutral means it has not been checked. These are capability hints; payment preparation still validates the service and wallet. The connected NWC wallet takes priority; external-wallet and copy actions remain available in recipient overflow menus. Optional signed-message controls stay collapsed until requested. Review boost prepares invoices without paying, then one final confirmation shows the exact recipients and amounts before sending. Recipients your connected wallet cannot pay remain visible in red as not paid. Their shares are redistributed among supported podcast recipients in proportion to their original published weights; the full chosen amount is allocated and the configured Woopod share stays fixed. Both review and final confirmation explain this. No supported podcast recipient means no payment. A service failure or uncertain payment never triggers automatic redistribution or a retry. On this device, payment review can acknowledge an interrupted split after you check your wallet; unknown outcomes stay unknown and unattempted shares are cancelled without retrying. For a recorded completed split, episode comments can offer an explicit public boost post without making another payment. After explicit confirmation, the payment flow can send the supported split shown in review, with any wallet-unsupported recipients excluded and the publisher portion reweighted proportionally. A signed boost message requires a compatible recipient and discussion; otherwise the payments do not carry a signed message. Direct node payments without custom routing records are available when the wallet advertises keysend; nodes requiring routing records need a validated invoice alternative in the published name or are excluded with an explanation; signed node boost messages remain unsupported. Real-wallet compatibility is still being tested.

Partial

Publisher comments

Tap the chat icon in Player or Episode comments in an episode’s details. Feed-provided discussion links choose the publisher’s official conversation. Woopod reads public Mastodon-compatible posts and replies with loading, retry, refresh and back-to-top controls. Content warnings hide the post text until revealed. Replies open on the original service using your account there. Other services use external links; feeds without discussion metadata explain that no thread is available. Publisher-disabled comments are respected. This is separate from the standard, public Nostr conversation shared with compatible podcast apps.

Available

Readable public conversations

Comments use nearly the whole Android screen. Replies are collapsed beneath their parent and can be written inline. New show and episode comments keep their identifiers in tags without appending an audio link to the text; clip posts retain their clip link. You can mute other authors, but not your own account. Authors show verified public profile names and pictures; tap one for their biography. Profile mentions and referenced notes open in-app previews, with browser fallback for unavailable records. Public notes and replies use the connected account only after an explicit posting action.

Transfer, data & independence5 capabilities
Available

OPML in and out

Import nested subscription files or export a standard OPML 2.0 document through Android’s picker. When importing, optionally mark existing episodes in newly imported shows as played; new episodes remain unplayed and automatic downloads skip played episodes. Already subscribed shows are unchanged. Duplicates are skipped; large imports checkpoint progress and can resume.

Available

Data backup & relay backup

Save settings, subscriptions, per-show options, Playlist, progress and bookmarks as a compressed JSON file or an encrypted account-bound Woopod relay backup. Local files are not encrypted; keep them safe. Audio, artwork, font files, cached episode lists and private keys are excluded. Restore asks for confirmation and finishes after reopening Woopod. Relay backups are limited to 90 MB after compression.

Available

Large-library storage

Podcast and episode records live in incremental SQLite storage rather than one giant preference value. Library opens from small saved headers and accurate episode counts; episode archives load in the background, with direct loading when a podcast is opened. Migration is transactional, and header-only metadata edits retain episode catalogs.

Available

No required Google runtime

The app includes no Google identity, Firebase, ad or tracking SDK. AndroidX, Media3, system DownloadManager and WorkManager do not require a Google account to run.

05 / Independence

Your phone.
Your feeds.
Your choice.

Woopod is designed to work without an account gate, ad network or required Google runtime. Public profile actions are separate from local listening. Nothing is published merely because you pressed play.

  • Direct publisher feedsRSS and Atom stay usable without a directory.
  • Private by defaultProgress, queue, settings and downloads remain local.
  • Portable subscriptionsStandard OPML import and export through your chosen storage.
